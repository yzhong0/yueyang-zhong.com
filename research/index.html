Research

Research interest

Human-centered operations. I study how people respond to operational policies, how those responses can be learned from data, and how that understanding can be turned into better designs for high-stakes service systems. My work takes classical stochastic modeling machinery to systems whose participants are strategic and whose parameters are unknown, then carries those models into domains where the stakes are real. Three commitments guide my research: endogenize behavior rather than assume it away; close the loop between theory and evidence; and address problems where better design can make a meaningful difference.

Topics

  1. (I)Behavioral and strategic queueing: How do customers and service providers respond to operational policies and to one another? I study these interactions and their implications for staffing, compensation, admission, and pricing.
  2. (II)Learning in queues: I use structural insights from queueing theory to develop online learning algorithms that make effective operational decisions while learning unknown system parameters.
  3. (III)High-stakes operations in congested systems: I examine how operational decisions shape efficiency, quality, and equity in healthcare, childcare, and other essential services where capacity constraints and congestion have significant human consequences.
  4. (IV)Information and mechanism design: I study how information, incentives, and rules can be designed to improve outcomes in areas such as mental health, education, and emerging technologies.

Behavior-Aware Queueing: The Finite-Buffer Setting with Many Strategic Servers

Yueyang Zhong, Raga Gopalakrishnan and Amy R. Ward

Operations Research 2025, 73(1), 290–310

  • AwardFinalist, 2022 INFORMS IBM Best Student Paper Award
  • CoverageChicago Booth Review
  • CoverageSmith Business Insight

DOISSRNTechnical supplement (SSRN)

Summary

When servers choose their own pace, managers must staff enough or pay enough. In a game-theoretic many-server queueing model, servers choose how fast to work in response to managerial decisions: how many servers to staff, how much to pay them, and whether to turn customers away. To control lost demand, waiting and burnout predictably, the manager must either staff enough servers or pay them enough; otherwise, loading underpaid servers past a tipping point triggers rebellion and a sharp drop in performance.

Some Asymptotic Properties of the Erlang-C Formula in Many-Server Limiting Regimes

Raga Gopalakrishnan and Yueyang Zhong

Operations Research Letters 2024, 54, 107116

DOIarXiv

Summary

This paper pins down how the classical delay-probability formula behaves as service systems grow large, closing a gap for critically loaded systems and extending square-root staffing. The Erlang-C formula, the probability that an arriving customer must wait, is a cornerstone of staffing and optimization problems in many-server queueing systems. The analysis works out its asymptotic behavior across the spectrum of many-server limiting regimes; the critically loaded case, where the literature had no limiting value, is settled by studying extensions of the square-root safety staffing rule familiar from the Quality-and-Efficiency-Driven regime.

Asymptotically Optimal Idling in the GI/GI/N+GI Queue

Yueyang Zhong, Amy R. Ward and Amber L. Puha

Operations Research Letters 2022, 50(3), 362–369

DOI

Summary

Keeping every server busy is not always optimal: when utilization itself is costly, turning some arrivals away so servers can idle is asymptotically best. The setting is a control problem for a many-server queue with general arrivals, service times and patience, trading off long-run average abandonment and holding costs against the cost of server utilization. As arrival rate and server count grow large, the associated fluid control problem shows that non-idling disciplines are not in general optimal; an admission-control policy that guarantees servers sufficient idle time is asymptotically optimal.

When Strategic Customers Meet Strategic Servers: Individual and Social Optimization in Many-Server Queueing Systems

Yueyang Zhong, Raga Gopalakrishnan and Amy R. Ward

Major revision at Operations Research

SSRN

Summary

When customers and servers are both strategic in the same many-server queue, the conclusions differ fundamentally from models where only one side is. Earlier work lets either customers decide whether to join or servers decide how hard to work, never both at once. This paper initiates the study of joint strategic behavior by customers and servers in the same system, contrasting individually optimal with socially optimal outcomes.

Racing Queues with Strategic Servers

Andrew Frazelle and Yueyang Zhong

Submitted

SSRN

Summary

When servers race for a winner-takes-all prize, adding a competitor can lower effort so much that the extra server adds no capacity. In a racing queue, strategic servers work on the same job; the first to finish earns a prize and all move to the next job, as in cryptocurrency mining or bug bounties. Serving faster wins more often but costs more. Equilibrium service rates are derived in closed form across overloaded, critically loaded and underloaded regimes. Effort can be non-monotone in server count, revealing an ideal level of competition. With the prize endogenized, the profit-maximizing prize sits systematically below the surplus-maximizing one, with relative surplus loss uniformly bounded.

Learning to Schedule in Multiclass Many-Server Queues with Abandonment

Yueyang Zhong, John R. Birge and Amy R. Ward

Operations Research 2025, 73(6), 3085–3103

  • CoverageChicago Booth Review
  • CoverageLondon Business School Research Brief Video

DOISSRN

Summary

Estimate the unknowns first, then schedule with a simple index rule: the gap to the full-information benchmark grows only logarithmically over time. The setting is a multiclass many-server queue with abandonment in which service and patience rates are unknown, and the question is which customer class a newly free server should take next. The Learn-then-Schedule policy first estimates the unknown primitives from observed data, then schedules by the simple, asymptotically optimal index rule. Its logarithmic regret is the best achievable rate.

Data-Driven Market-Making via Model-Free Learning

Yueyang Zhong, YeeMan Bergstrom and Amy Ward

IJCAI 2020, 4461–4468

DOI

Summary

A model-free reinforcement-learning strategy for placing orders, simple enough to run from a lookup table, beat benchmark strategies in a collaborating market-making firm's backtester. The question is when a market-making firm should place orders on a limit order book to maximize net profit while constraining risk. The paper applies Q-learning, a model-free, off-policy method, with state aggregation, to produce a trading strategy implementable as a simple lookup table. Trained on event-by-event order-book data, the strategy passed in-sample and out-of-sample testing in the firm's backtester and outperformed the benchmarks; the firm sought to put it into production.

Dispatching for Efficiency and Fairness in Emergency Medical Service Systems

Jinghai He (student at the time), Cheng Hua, Yueyang Zhong and Tauhid Zaman

In preparation for submission

  • AwardFirst Prize, 2024 INFORMS Conference on Service Science Best Paper

Manuscript available on request

Summary

On real data from St. Paul, Minnesota, a fairness-aware learned dispatch policy beats sending the nearest ambulance, gaining substantial fairness with minimal loss of efficiency. Dispatching the nearest available ambulance is standard practice, and short-sighted. The paper formulates emergency dispatching as a fairness-aware average-reward dynamic program, using α-fairness to weigh efficiency against equitable access across regions, and solves it with a temporal-difference learning algorithm with guaranteed convergence. The learned policy beats nearest-unit dispatch on five efficiency and fairness metrics, and stays robust to time-varying arrivals and non-Markovian service times.

Which Experiments Can You Trust? Cluster-Robust Causal Reinforcement Learning

Cong Zhang and Yueyang Zhong

In preparation for submission

  • NoteA shorter version has been submitted to NeurIPS.

Manuscript available on request

Summary

When platforms learn policies from A/B tests, some of them contaminated, trusting only the experiments that agree with each other recovers the full gain an oracle would. Platforms set levers like how aggressively a recommender curates a feed from logs where the lever responded to demand shocks. A/B tests are natural instruments, but bundled or overlapping treatments can move the reward directly. The method fits one value function per experiment and combines the largest group whose fitted functions agree. With four of ten simulated experiments contaminated, it captures the full oracle gain; pooled instrumental-variable Q-learning captures 10%.

Is Continuity All We Need? A Modeling Approach to Evaluating Relational Continuity in Primary Care

Naireet Ghosh (student at the time), Nicos Savva and Yueyang Zhong

Major revision at Management Science

  • AwardFinalist, Pierskalla Best Paper Competition, 2026
  • AwardAccepted to MSOM Healthcare SIG, 2026

SSRN

Summary

Continuity with one's own physician competes with fast access; a sequence of queueing models says when continuity pays, how much, and who should decide. Primary care faces this trade-off between relational continuity, seeing the same physician over time, and access, being seen quickly. Models of increasing sophistication work out when continuity should be used, how much of it is operationally optimal, and whether the choice should be made centrally by providers or left to patients.

Reserving Capacity, Restoring Fairness: Scheduling Elective Caesarean Sections in the English NHS

Yuhang Du (student at the time), Yueyang Zhong, Catherine Aiken∗ (industrial collaborator) and Pedra Rabiee∗ (industrial collaborator)

Submitted

  • CoverageLondon Business School Think
  • ImpactPilot study underway at Cambridge University Hospitals since July 2026.

SSRN

Summary

No scheduling rule can equalize emergency diversions of elective caesareans between early- and late-identified patients in the English NHS, but reserving theatre capacity for early-identified patients can cut them sharply. A fluid queueing model with two-sided clinical windows yields the optimal reservation fraction. Calibrated to Cambridge University Hospitals, reserving 10 of 18 weekly slots cuts late-patient diversions from 22% to 10%, confirms dates for about 70% of early patients at clinical request, and lowers case-loss cost by 8.9% (£15,300 per year, more than £1.9M NHS-wide), all without additional theatre capacity. The rule operationalizes the RCOG 2025 guidance calling for dedicated elective caesarean sessions sized to demand.

Socioeconomic Deprivation and Risk of Early-Onset Pre-eclampsia in England: A National Population-Based Cohort Study

Ethan Phillips (student at the time), Federica Caretta Cortegiani (student at the time), Catherine Aiken∗ (industrial collaborator), Marian Knight∗ (industrial collaborator), Harshita Kajaria-Montag, Agni Orfanoudaki and Yueyang Zhong

Submitted to BMJ Public Health

  • CoverageWheeler Institute Research Brief
  • FundingSupported in part by funding from the Wheeler Institute for Business and Development at London Business School.
  • FundingSupported in part by funding from the UKRI Knowledge Exchange Fund.

medRxiv

Summary

In a retrospective national cohort of 1,027,707 first-time mothers in NHS maternity care in England, 2021–2025, each point of neighborhood deprivation carried 3.4% higher odds of early-onset pre-eclampsia; 940,505 multiparous women were analyzed secondarily. Adjusted for confounders, each one-point rise on a 0–10 deprivation score gave an odds ratio of 1.034; adjusting for theorized mediators attenuated this modestly (1.023) and for hospital site further (1.016), so both individual risk factors and maternity-site variation contribute. Elevated BMI, circulatory disease, maternal age over 40, Black ethnicity and endocrine or metabolic disease were among the strongest predictors. Associations were similar but stronger among multiparous women, and weak or absent for late-onset pre-eclampsia.

Phantom Waitlists in Childcare

Yueyang Zhong, Jun Li and Senthil Veeraraghavan

Work in progress

Manuscript available on request

Summary

Childcare waitlists are long while slots sit empty because stale 'phantom' listings clog them; regular verification and mixed-age classrooms expand effective capacity. Families join waitlists under enrollment uncertainty and leave phantom listings that look active after they have found care elsewhere; centers discover which listings are live only by making an offer and waiting, so parents wait months while licensed slots sit vacant. Two remedies are studied: verification that clears phantom listings before offers are made, and mixed-age classrooms that let older-child slots serve younger children, raising utilization and shortening waits.

AI-Risk Disclosure Is Not One-Size-Fits-All: An Agentic Measurement System and Network Evidence

Tianhui Li (student at the time) and Yueyang Zhong

Submitted

  • FundingSupported in part by funding from the LBS Data Science and AI Initiative.

SSRN

Summary

Across 11,271 LinkedIn posts, the same AI-risk disclosure signal tracks sharply different financial patterns depending on a firm's place in the AI value chain: disclosure is not one-size-fits-all. An auditable, agentic measurement system extracts the signal from the posts and relates it to firms' positions in the AI value chain and their business networks. The result gives managers and regulators a practical early-warning system for monitoring AI-risk communication, and a clear reason not to impose uniform disclosure expectations.

Continuity of Care on Mental Health Platforms: A Field Experiment on Behavioral Nudges

Guang Cheng (student at the time), Sidika Candogan and Yueyang Zhong

In preparation for submission

  • FundingSupported in part by funding from the Wheeler Institute for Business and Development at London Business School.

Manuscript available on request

Summary

Nudge emails on a mental-health platform had no average effect on follow-up bookings; exploratory analysis finds a simple prompt helps clients early in therapy. On Safe Space, a Southeast Asian platform, a pre-registered randomized field experiment tested three nudge emails: a basic prompt, a social-information nudge, and a research-based nudge. Broadcast to eligible clients, none moved bookings on average. Among clients early in therapy the basic nudge beats richer content, so targeting simple nudges early may generate more bookings with fewer emails than broadcasting.
